The leather drive belt was a vital link between a power source and the machines of a workshop or factory. Its importance lies in the interaction of power generation, transmission and production.

From Power Source to Working Machine

Waterwheels, and later steam engines, could drive several machines through shafts and pulleys. A single drive belt transferred rotation between two pulleys, while long line shafts distributed power throughout the building.

Industrial Transformation

19th Century
Centralised power transmission became widespread in workshops and factories; leather belts were an important technical solution.
Around 1900
Mature line-shaft systems existed alongside other belt materials and increasing electrification.
20th Century
Individual electric motors reduced the need for extensive line-shaft systems in many places. Specialised applications continued.

Krafft as a Historical Example

Belt production was already part of Gebrüder Krafft's operations in the second half of the 19th century. The 1929 anniversary publication describes the development of the factory's power supply and depicts production rooms. Individual drive installations can only be dated from the written evidence, not from the photographs alone.
